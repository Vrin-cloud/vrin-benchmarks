### Step 3 — Pairwise Business Overlap & Downturn Correlation

**Tier 1 — Highest Overlap (Would Correlate Most in a Downturn):**

**GOOGL + META** — This is your most dangerous correlation. GOOGL derives 72% of its revenue from advertising (Search + YouTube + Network), and META derives 98.5% from advertising. In an ad market recession — which historically happens during economic downturns as marketing budgets are among the first discretionary items cut — both companies would see simultaneous revenue compression. They compete for the same brand and performance advertising budgets, and their combined exposure means 34% of your equal-weight portfolio is tied to a single macro variable: *advertiser willingness to spend*. The GOOGL 10-K explicitly notes a "revenue mix shift from Google Network properties to Google Search" — when advertisers cut spend, they consolidate toward higher-ROI channels, which means Network revenue (lower margin) falls first, compressing GOOGL's margins faster than the headline revenue decline suggests.

**Tier 2 — Significant Overlap:**

**MSFT + AMZN + GOOGL (Cloud Triad)** — All three derive meaningful revenue from cloud infrastructure (MSFT ~37%, AMZN ~18%, GOOGL ~15% of their respective revenues). Enterprise IT spending is cyclical. In a recession, enterprises delay cloud migration projects, optimize existing workloads, and negotiate harder on pricing. All three clouds saw this during the 2022-2023 optimization cycle. Your portfolio has ~14% exposed to this single spending category, and the three companies would move in tandem during an enterprise spending pullback.

**AMZN + GOOGL + META (Advertising Overlap)** — While AMZN's advertising is structurally different (retail-intent, high-conversion) versus GOOGL/META (brand + discovery), all three ultimately compete for the same pool of digital ad dollars. AMZN's ad business ($68.5B, growing 22% YoY) is increasingly cannibalizing spend from the other two — but in a severe downturn, even AMZN ads would contract as sellers reduce sponsored product budgets.

**Tier 3 — Indirect/Structural Linkage:**

**AAPL + GOOGL (TAC Dependency)** — Google pays Apple an estimated $20-26B annually in traffic acquisition costs for default search placement on iOS. This means a portion of AAPL's high-margin Services revenue is directly dependent on GOOGL's Search advertising revenue. If the DOJ antitrust remedy restricts or eliminates default search agreements, both companies are impacted — AAPL loses a major Services revenue stream, and GOOGL loses privileged access to iOS search queries. This is a regulatory correlation, not a market correlation, but it's significant.

**AAPL + AMZN (Consumer Discretionary)** — AAPL's hardware (74% of revenue) and AMZN's retail (65% of revenue) are both sensitive to consumer spending. In a recession, iPhone upgrade cycles lengthen and Amazon consumer spending shifts toward essentials, hitting both simultaneously.

### Step 4 — Actionable Portfolio Insight

**Your portfolio's hidden risk is advertising concentration.** A naïve equal-weight allocation across these five names looks diversified — hardware, cloud, e-commerce, social media. But when you decompose by revenue function, 37% of your effective exposure is to digital advertising, and it's concentrated in two names that compete in the same market. 

If you're looking to reduce correlated downside risk, the most impactful moves would be: (1) underweight the GOOGL-META pair relative to the other three, since they represent your largest single-factor exposure; or (2) if maintaining equal weight, recognize that your portfolio has approximately 1.8x the advertising revenue exposure you'd intuitively expect — and hedge accordingly around ad-market cycle indicators like the CMO Survey or agency holding company earnings.

The MSFT-AMZN-GOOGL cloud overlap is a secondary concern, but it's mitigated by the fact that cloud is still a structural growth market with enterprise IT spend shifting from on-premise, which provides a floor even in cyclical downturns (cloud revenue declined in *growth rate* during 2022-2023 but never actually contracted in absolute terms for any of the three).
