## Q2: AI/Cloud Infrastructure Capex — Who's Spending the Most?

**Important correction to Q1:** While extracting the META FCF reconciliation table directly from the 10-K, I confirmed the column ordering is 2025 → 2024 → 2023. META's FCF actually **declined** from $52.1B (FY2024) to $43.6B (FY2025), a −16.3% drop driven by a near-doubling of property & equipment purchases. I apologize for the reversal in Q1 — META is not an FCF grower but rather the most aggressive reinvestor of the group.

---

### FY2025 Capex as a Percentage of Revenue

| Company | FY2025 Capex (incl. finance leases) | FY2024 Capex | YoY Growth | FY2025 Revenue | **Capex / Revenue** |
|---------|------|------|------|------|------|
| **META** | $72.2B | $39.2B | **+84%** | $201.0B | **35.9%** |
| **MSFT** | ~$64.6B | ~$44.5B | +45% | $281.7B | **22.9%** |
| **GOOGL** | ~$85.0B | ~$52.5B | +62% | $402.8B | **21.1%** |
| **AMZN** | $131.8B | $83.0B | +59% | $716.9B | **18.4%** |
| **AAPL** | ~$12.7B | ~$9.9B | +28% | $416.2B | **3.1%** |

**META is the clear leader at 35.9% of revenue going to capex** — more than 10 percentage points above the next-closest hyperscaler. This is striking because META is not a cloud services provider; it's spending this to build AI training/inference capacity for its ad recommendation engine, generative AI features, and Reality Labs.

From META's 10-K: purchases of property and equipment surged from $37.3B (FY2024) to **$69.7B** (FY2025), plus $2.5B in finance lease payments. The company also disclosed **$81.2B in non-cancelable contractual commitments** as of September 2025, mostly for cloud capacity and server infrastructure — signaling that the spending wave is far from over.

### 2026 Forward Guidance — The Spending Supercycle Accelerates

From earnings calls and SEC filings sourced via VRIN:

| Company | 2026 Capex Guidance | Implied YoY Growth | Est. Capex / Revenue |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **AMZN** | ~$200B | +52% | ~25% |
| **GOOGL** | $175–185B | +106–118% | ~38–40% |
| **MSFT** | ~$145B (run-rate) | +125% | ~44% |
| **META** | $115–135B | +59–87% | ~47–55% |
| **AAPL** | Not disclosed | — | ~3–4% |

Key management quotes from VRIN:

- **META** (Q4 FY2025 call): *"We anticipate 2026 capital expenditures, including principal payments on finance leases, to be in the range of $115-135 billion, with year-over-year growth driven by increased investment to support our Meta Superintelligence Labs efforts and core business."* Management also noted: *"Despite the meaningful step up in infrastructure investment, in 2026 we expect to deliver operating income that is above 2025 operating income."*

- **MSFT** (Q2 FY2026 call, Amy Hood): *"Capital expenditures were $37.5 billion, and this quarter, roughly two-thirds of our CapEx was on short-lived assets, primarily GPUs and CPUs. Our customer demand continues to exceed our supply."* At $37.5B per quarter, the annualized run-rate is ~$150B.

- **GOOGL** (10-Q, Sep 2025): *"In 2026, we expect to significantly increase, relative to 2025, our investment in our technical infrastructure, including servers, network equipment, and data centers, to support the growth of our business."*

Combined, the four hyperscalers are guiding toward **~$635–665B in 2026 capex** — a roughly **67–74% spike** from $381B in 2025. Approximately 75% of this (~$450B) is directly tied to AI infrastructure.

---

### Actionable Investment Insight

**META is the most aggressive spender relative to its revenue base**, and this is a double-edged sword. At 36% capex-to-revenue in FY2025 rising to potentially 47–55% in FY2026, META is making the largest bet proportionally on the AI infrastructure supercycle. If AI-driven ad revenue continues to compound at 20%+, this investment will look prescient. If ad growth decelerates, the fixed-cost overhang becomes punishing — as the FCF decline from $52.1B to $43.6B already demonstrates.

**AMZN's capex is the largest in absolute terms** ($131.8B → ~$200B) and has already cratered FCF to just $7.7B. This is a familiar Amazon playbook — sacrifice near-term cash flow for long-term AWS dominance — but the sheer magnitude ($200B in a single year) raises the stakes considerably.

**AAPL is the outlier**, spending only 3% of revenue on capex. This reflects its asset-light hardware model (contract manufacturing) and its decision to rely on partner cloud infrastructure for Apple Intelligence rather than building its own GPU clusters.
