### The Answer: Microsoft — The Only Company With Declining Gross Margins

**MSFT is the only company in the group with a sustained gross margin decline**: 71.4% → 69.8% → 68.8%, a cumulative 260 basis point erosion over two years. The 10-K is explicit about the cause:

> *"Gross margin percentage decreased slightly driven by continued investments in AI infrastructure"*

> *"Microsoft Cloud gross margin percentage decreased to 69% driven by the impact of scaling our AI infrastructure, offset in part by efficiency gains in Azure."*

However, there's a critical nuance: **MSFT's formal R&D line as a percentage of revenue is actually declining** (12.8% → 12.0% → 11.5%) because revenue growth (+15% YoY) outpaces R&D spending growth (+10% YoY). On the surface, this makes MSFT look like it's becoming *more* efficient on R&D, not less.

---

### The Deeper Insight: AI Investment Is Migrating from R&D to COGS

This is where the analysis gets interesting — and why the traditional "R&D as % of revenue" metric is increasingly misleading for hyperscalers.

**MSFT's gross margin decline is itself a form of R&D investment.** The AI infrastructure depreciation that compresses gross margins (GPU clusters, data centers) is functionally the same type of spending as R&D — it's investment in AI capability. But it flows through **cost of revenue** (as depreciation and cloud infrastructure operating costs) rather than the R&D expense line.

Let me quantify this "hidden R&D":

| Component | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|-----------|--------|--------|--------|
| Formal R&D | $29,510M | $32,488M | +$2,978M (+10.1%) |
| Cost of Revenue | $74,114M | $87,831M | +$13,717M (+18.5%) |
| **Combined "Innovation Spend"** | **$103,624M** | **$120,319M** | **+$16,695M (+16.1%)** |
| As % of Revenue | **42.3%** | **42.7%** | **+40 bps** |

When you combine formal R&D with cost of revenue, MSFT's total "innovation spending" as a percentage of revenue is actually **increasing** — from 42.3% to 42.7%. The AI infrastructure investment just shows up in a different line item than where analysts traditionally look.

This is a structural accounting shift happening across all hyperscalers: as AI models require massive GPU clusters that are capitalized and then depreciated through COGS, the income statement is separating "where AI money is spent" (COGS) from "where it's labeled" (R&D). MSFT is simply further along this curve than the others.

---

### Why GOOGL and AMZN Are Next

GOOGL and AMZN haven't yet seen gross margin compression because their revenue growth has — so far — outpaced cost-of-revenue growth. But the inflection point is approaching:

| Company | Cost of Rev Growth (FY24→25) | Revenue Growth (FY24→25) | Gap |
|---------|----------------------------|-------------------------|-----|
| **MSFT** | +18.5% | +14.9% | **Cost outpacing revenue by 3.6pp** |
| **GOOGL** | +11.1% | +15.1% | Revenue still ahead by 4.0pp |
| **AMZN** | +9.2% | +12.4% | Revenue still ahead by 3.2pp |

For GOOGL specifically, "other cost of revenues" (primarily AI infrastructure depreciation) surged $11.2B in FY2025, and R&D jumped to 15.2% of revenue (up 110 bps YoY). GOOGL is the closest to crossing the same threshold MSFT has already crossed. If GOOGL's cost of revenue growth accelerates above its ~15% top-line growth rate — plausible given the $85B→$175B+ capex ramp — gross margins will begin compressing in FY2026.

---

### What This Tells Us About Competitive Position

**MSFT's margin compression signals the cost of defending its cloud leadership position.** Azure is growing 31% YoY (with 13pp from AI services), and Microsoft is accepting margin erosion to lock in AI infrastructure capacity ahead of demand. This is a classic market-share consolidation strategy: sacrifice near-term profitability to build a structural moat.

The competitive implication is significant. MSFT's willingness to absorb 260 bps of margin compression means competitors must match this investment to remain relevant — but not all can afford to. AAPL (at only 3% capex/revenue) is deliberately opting out of this race; it's an AI consumer, not an AI infrastructure builder. This is a fundamentally different strategic bet.

**For investors:** MSFT's declining gross margins should not be read as deteriorating business quality. They should be read as the price of admission to the AI platform war — one that MSFT is currently winning (Azure AI is growing faster than any competitor). The question is whether AI-driven revenue acceleration will eventually re-expand margins, or whether infrastructure costs become permanently elevated. The 10-K's own language — "offset in part by efficiency gains in Azure" — suggests MSFT expects to bend the cost curve eventually as utilization improves.
