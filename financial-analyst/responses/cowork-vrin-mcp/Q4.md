## Q4: Amazon Cash Flow Deep Dive — The $62B Gap Between Net Income and Operating Cash Flow

---

### The Big Picture: Three-Year Cash Flow Trajectory

From Amazon's 10-K (filed February 5, 2026), here are the consolidated cash flow figures directly from the SEC filing:

| Line Item (in millions) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Net Income** | $30,425 | $59,248 | $77,670 | **+31.1%** |
| **Operating Cash Flow** | $84,946 | $115,877 | $139,514 | **+20.4%** |
| **Gap (OCF − Net Income)** | $54,521 | $56,629 | $61,844 | **+$5.2B** |
| Purchases of P&E | ($52,729) | ($82,999) | ($131,819) | **+58.8%** |
| P&E Proceeds & Incentives | $4,596 | $5,341 | $3,499 | −34.5% |
| **Net Capex** | ($48,133) | ($77,658) | ($128,320) | +65.2% |
| **Free Cash Flow (OCF − Gross Capex)** | $32,217 | $32,878 | **$7,695** | **−76.6%** |

The headline story: Amazon's net income nearly tripled from FY2023 to FY2025 ($30.4B → $77.7B), while free cash flow collapsed from $32.2B to just $7.7B. This divergence is driven by two separate dynamics — a massive non-cash adjustment bridge on the operating side, and an infrastructure spending explosion on the investing side.

---

### Part 1: What's Driving the $61.8B Gap Between Net Income and OCF

The $61.8B gap between net income ($77.7B) and operating cash flow ($139.5B) in FY2025 is the result of large non-cash charges being added back, partially offset by working capital absorption. Here are the key components:

**Non-Cash Add-Backs (~$88–90B):**

| Adjustment | FY2024 (est.) | FY2025 (est.) | What It Represents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depreciation & Amortization** | ~$48B | ~$56B | Wear-down of $318B gross PP&E base (servers, fulfillment centers, data centers, content) |
| **Stock-Based Compensation** | ~$24B | ~$29B | Non-cash employee comp; Amazon's workforce is ~1.5M+ |
| **Operating Lease Amortization** | ~$12B | ~$14B | Non-cash portion of lease payments for fulfillment/data center leases |
| **Other (deferred taxes, impairments, etc.)** | ~$3B | ~$5B | Includes $1.3B in asset impairments and $2.5B FTC settlement accrual in FY2025 |

**Working Capital Offsets (~$−27B):**

Amazon's rapid growth requires increasing working capital investment. Growing receivables (seller/advertising revenue), inventory build-outs, and timing of payables can consume $25–30B per year in cash. This partially offsets the non-cash add-backs, netting the total adjustment to $61.8B.

The D&A figure is the dominant driver and deserves special attention. Amazon disclosed accumulated depreciation of **$177.1B** at year-end 2025 versus $141.4B at year-end 2024 — a **$35.7B increase** just from the PP&E depreciation charge (before accounting for asset retirements). When you add content cost amortization (~$15–18B for Prime Video, music, etc.) and intangibles amortization, total D&A approaches $56B.

From the 10-K: *"Depreciation and amortization expense on property and equipment, including corporate property and equipment, are allocated to all segments based on usage."* The useful-life change made in Q2 2025 — shortening certain server lives — actually **increased** depreciation by an additional $889M and reduced net income by $677M, primarily impacting the AWS segment.

---

### Part 2: Why Free Cash Flow Collapsed

The FCF story is entirely a capex story. Operating cash flow actually grew a healthy 20.4% ($115.9B → $139.5B). But capital expenditures exploded:

| Capex Component | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross Purchases of P&E** | $52.7B | $83.0B | **$131.8B** |
| P&E Proceeds/Incentives | ($4.6B) | ($5.3B) | ($3.5B) |
| **Net P&E** | $48.1B | $77.7B | **$128.3B** |

From VRIN, cash capital expenditures for the first nine months of 2025 alone were $89.9B (versus $51.6B in the same period of 2024), which *"primarily reflect investments in technology infrastructure (the majority of which is to support AWS business growth) and in additional capacity to support our fulfillment network."*

Adding finance lease obligations makes the picture worse. Amazon's gross finance lease assets were $55.6B (FY2025) with $40.4B accumulated amortization, and the company continues entering new finance leases for data center equipment. When you include principal repayments on finance leases and financing obligations, Amazon's "all-in" FCF (their supplemental non-GAAP measure) likely approached **break-even or negative** for FY2025.

---

### Part 3: Notable One-Time Items in FY2025

The 10-K disclosed several non-recurring charges that further distort the income-to-cash-flow relationship:

- **FTC Settlement**: $2.5B recorded in Q3 2025 in "Other operating expense" — a cash outflow that hit the income statement but may not have been fully paid in cash yet (partially accrued)
- **Severance Costs**: ~$2.7B related to planned role eliminations ($1.8B in Q3, $730M in Q4) — cash charge that's an operating item
- **Asset Impairments**: $1.3B in FY2025 ($610M in Q4), primarily property and operating leases — non-cash charge that reduces net income but doesn't consume cash
- **Server Useful-Life Change**: Shortened certain server lives, adding $889M in depreciation — non-cash, reduces income but increases the OCF adjustment

### Actionable Investment Insight

Amazon's cash flow profile in FY2025 illustrates the classic "earnings power vs. investment cycle" tension. The *underlying business* generated $139.5B in operating cash flow — a record — and net income of $77.7B, both at all-time highs. The FCF collapse is entirely a **management choice** to reinvest at historic levels ($131.8B capex), not a deterioration in business fundamentals.

The critical question is: **what is the marginal return on this incremental $49B of capex** ($131.8B vs. $83.0B prior year)?

If the investment primarily supports AWS growth (which carries ~37% operating margins vs. ~6% for retail), then every dollar of incremental AWS revenue generated from this capex creates significantly more operating profit than the retail business. AWS grew 19% YoY to $128.7B in FY2025 and is accelerating. If AWS can sustain 20%+ growth through 2027 thanks to this infrastructure, the FY2025 capex will look visionary in hindsight.

The bear case: if AI infrastructure demand plateaus or AWS growth decelerates below 15%, Amazon would be sitting on $130B+ of annual capex commitments generating diminishing returns — and FCF could turn outright negative.

Watch Q1 2026 capital intensity (capex/revenue) as the leading indicator. If it remains above 18%, the reinvestment thesis is still the dominant narrative. If it begins declining toward 15%, it would signal the investment peak is behind us and FCF recovery is underway.

Ready for **Q5**.
