## Q3: Top 3 NEW Risk Factors in META's FY2025 10-K

After cross-referencing META's FY2025 10-K (filed March 2026) against prior filings, three risk factors stand out as materially new or dramatically escalated in prominence.

---

### Risk #1: AI & Superintelligence Investment Failure

**10-K language (new heading):**
> *"We may not be successful in our artificial intelligence initiatives, which could adversely affect our business, reputation, or financial results. We have made significant investments in AI initiatives, including generative AI and superintelligence..."*

> *"If our new products or changes to existing products fail to engage users, marketers, or developers, or if our business plans are unsuccessful, we may fail to attract or retain users or to generate sufficient revenue, operating margin, or other value to justify our investments."*

**Why this is new:** Prior 10-K filings (FY2023 and earlier) mentioned AI as a general technology risk within broader product development disclosures. The FY2025 filing elevates this to a **standalone, named risk factor** — and critically, adds "superintelligence" for the first time, reflecting the January 2025 creation of **Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL)** under Yann LeCun and Ahmad Al-Dahle. The business change driving this is the sheer scale of the bet: $72.2B in FY2025 capex (+84% YoY) with $115–135B guided for FY2026, explicitly earmarked for "Meta Superintelligence Labs efforts and core business." Management is essentially acknowledging in regulatory language that a generational capital commitment is being made on an unproven technology trajectory.

**Investment implication:** This risk factor is management's formal hedge against the possibility that their AI models (Llama 4, etc.) fail to monetize at a rate that justifies the infrastructure. If Meta's AI-driven ad recommendation improvements plateau, the company would be sitting on $200B+ of rapidly depreciating infrastructure with no proportional revenue uplift.

---

### Risk #2: Infrastructure Overbuilding & Asset Impairment

**10-K language (expanded):**
> *"Charges associated with impairment or abandonment of any assets on our balance sheet, including as a result of changes to our real property lease arrangements and data center and infrastructure assets"*

> *"There may be issues related to this infrastructure that are not identified during the testing phases of design and implementation, which may only become evident after we have started to fully utilize the underlying equipment, that could further degrade the user experience or increase our costs."*

**Accounting signal — useful life change:**
> *"In January 2025, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of property and equipment, which resulted in an increase..."*

This assessment extended server useful lives (likely from 4 to 5–6 years), reducing near-term depreciation expense. While it improved reported earnings, it's a double-edged sword: longer useful lives mean the assets stay on the books longer, increasing impairment risk if the equipment becomes technologically obsolete.

**Why this is new:** Prior filings mentioned asset impairment as a generic risk. The FY2025 10-K dramatically amplifies this because:

- **$69.7B in property & equipment purchases** in a single year (nearly double the $37.3B in FY2024)
- **$81.2B in non-cancelable contractual commitments** as of September 2025, mostly for servers and cloud capacity
- **$58.1B in lease obligations** for data centers that haven't even commenced yet (commencing 2025–2030)
- The **5GW Hyperion data center campus** under development — a facility that alone could consume more power than many small countries

The combined exposure is staggering: META has committed roughly **$209B in future infrastructure obligations** ($72.2B actual FY2025 capex + $81.2B contracts + $58.1B leases). If GPU architecture shifts (e.g., a transition from NVIDIA H100/B200 to a fundamentally different compute paradigm), a significant portion of this could become stranded.

**Investment implication:** Watch depreciation expense closely. It was already $15.1B for the first nine months of FY2025 (+36% YoY). If META is forced to reverse the useful-life extension or take impairment charges, operating income could drop by $5–10B+ in a single quarter.

---

### Risk #3: Layered AI-Specific Regulatory Framework (EU AI Act + Youth Litigation)

**10-K language (new statute named):**
> *"...complex and evolving U.S. and foreign privacy, data use, data combination, data protection, content and content moderation, competition, youth, safety, consumer protection, advertising, and other laws and regulations"*

> *"(GDPR), Digital Markets Act (DMA), Digital Services Act (DSA), UK Online Safety Act (OSA), **Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act)**, and the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumer Act (DMCC)"*

> *"Several bellwether trials in our youth-related litigation matters are scheduled for 2026 and beyond."*

**Why this is new:** The **EU AI Act** is named for the first time in the FY2025 10-K. This law, which entered phased enforcement beginning February 2025, classifies certain AI systems by risk level and imposes strict compliance obligations on "high-risk" AI systems — which could encompass Meta's AI-driven content recommendation and ad-targeting algorithms. The Act's prohibition on certain AI practices (e.g., social scoring, real-time biometric identification) and its mandatory transparency requirements for general-purpose AI models like Llama create a compliance burden that didn't exist in prior filings.

Simultaneously, the **youth-related litigation risk** has escalated materially. META now discloses that bellwether trials are scheduled for 2026, and the Nevada AG is seeking to **enjoin default end-to-end encryption on Messenger** for residents under 18. This represents a shift from theoretical regulatory exposure to active courtroom liability.

**The convergence** of these two threads is what's new: META faces simultaneous regulatory pressure on (a) how its AI models operate (EU AI Act), (b) how they affect minors (youth litigation + state AGs), and (c) how they handle data (GDPR/DSA). Each regulation individually is manageable; the combination creates a compliance matrix where satisfying one requirement may conflict with another (e.g., encrypting messages for privacy vs. making them inspectable for child safety).

**Investment implication:** Budget $3–5B in incremental annual compliance costs as the EU AI Act fully phases in through 2027. The youth litigation bellwether trials in 2026 are a near-term catalyst — an adverse verdict could expose META to multi-billion-dollar liability across consolidated cases nationwide.

---

### Summary: What's Different About the FY2025 Risk Section

| Risk Factor | Prior Filings | FY2025 10-K | Business Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI / Superintelligence** | Generic R&D risk | Standalone named risk factor | Creation of MSL; $72B+ capex; Llama 4 |
| **Infrastructure Impairment** | Boilerplate asset risk | Amplified with $209B committed obligations | Capex doubled; 5GW campuses; useful-life change |
| **EU AI Act + Youth Litigation** | Not named | Explicitly named; bellwether trials disclosed | EU AI Act enforcement began Feb 2025; state AG actions |

The common thread across all three is **META's transformation from a social media company into an AI infrastructure company**. That strategic pivot — which redirected $72B of capital in a single year — fundamentally changed the risk profile of the business. The 10-K now reads more like a hyperscaler/semiconductor company than an ad-tech platform, which is itself a form of risk disclosure.
