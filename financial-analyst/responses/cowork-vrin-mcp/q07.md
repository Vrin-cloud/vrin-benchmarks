### Step 3 — Pairwise Business Overlap & Downturn Correlation

**Tier 1 — Highest Overlap:**

**GOOGL + META** — GOOGL derives 72% of revenue from advertising, META derives 98.5%. In an ad market recession, both see simultaneous revenue compression. They compete for the same brand and performance advertising budgets. Combined exposure: 34% of portfolio tied to *advertiser willingness to spend*. The GOOGL 10-K notes "revenue mix shift from Google Network properties to Google Search" — when advertisers cut spend, Network revenue falls first, compressing margins faster.

**Tier 2 — Significant Overlap:**

**MSFT + AMZN + GOOGL (Cloud Triad)** — All three derive meaningful revenue from cloud (MSFT ~37%, AMZN ~18%, GOOGL ~15%). Enterprise IT spending is cyclical. Portfolio has ~14% exposed to this category.

**AMZN + GOOGL + META (Advertising Overlap)** — While AMZN's ad business is structurally different (retail-intent, high-conversion), all three compete for the same digital ad dollars.

**Tier 3 — Indirect/Structural:**

**AAPL + GOOGL (TAC Dependency)** — Google pays Apple an estimated $20-26B annually for default search placement on iOS. If DOJ antitrust restricts default search agreements, both are impacted.

**AAPL + AMZN (Consumer Discretionary)** — AAPL's hardware (74% of revenue) and AMZN's retail (65%) are both sensitive to consumer spending.

---

### Step 4 — Actionable Portfolio Insight

**Your portfolio's hidden risk is advertising concentration.** If you're looking to reduce correlated downside risk: (1) underweight the GOOGL-META pair, or (2) hedge around ad-market cycle indicators like the CMO Survey or agency holding company earnings.

The MSFT-AMZN-GOOGL cloud overlap is a secondary concern, mitigated by cloud being a structural growth market (revenue declined in *growth rate* during 2022-2023 but never contracted in absolute terms).
