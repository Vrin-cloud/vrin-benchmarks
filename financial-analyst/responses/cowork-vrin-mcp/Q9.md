## Q9: Notable Inconsistencies Between 8-K Press Releases/Earnings Calls and 10-K Risk Factors/MD&A

This is the most analytically important question in the set, because disclosure inconsistencies are where material investment risk hides. After cross-referencing each company's most recent earnings press release (8-K) and earnings call transcript against their FY2025 10-K risk factors and MD&A, I found five notable inconsistencies — ranked from most material to least.

---

### Inconsistency #1 (Most Material): **MSFT — OpenAI as "Strategic Partner" on Earnings Calls vs. "Competitor" in the 10-K**

**What the 8-K/earnings call says:** Nadella celebrated the OpenAI relationship as a cornerstone of Microsoft's AI strategy: *"This quarter, the Microsoft Cloud surpassed $50 billion in revenue for the first time."* Amy Hood highlighted that the $625B RPO included a massive multiyear OpenAI contract. The earnings press release leads with Azure growth powered by "AI services including our partnership with OpenAI." The tone is unambiguously positive — OpenAI is presented as Microsoft's greatest competitive asset.

**What the 10-K says:** In the risk factors section, Microsoft now *lists OpenAI as a competitor* in AI offerings and in search and news advertising, alongside Amazon, Apple, Google, and Meta. The 10-K discloses that MSFT's investment in OpenAI generated net losses of $3.1B in Q1 FY2026 (and a $7.6B gain in Q2 due to revaluation — the volatility itself being the risk). The filing further notes that OpenAI has relationships with Amazon.com, Inc. (per VRIN: "OpenAI is_a_company_that_amazoncom_inc_has_relationships_with"), meaning the "exclusive" partnership narrative is more nuanced than the earnings call suggests.

**Why it matters:** This is the textbook 8-K vs. 10-K gap that securities lawyers warn about. On earnings calls, MSFT presents OpenAI as an extension of its own product suite. In the 10-K, the legal team simultaneously discloses that OpenAI is a competitor that could offer competing AI services through other platforms, that the investment has generated volatile mark-to-market swings ($3.1B loss followed by $7.6B gain), and that 45% of MSFT's $625B RPO backlog is attributable to OpenAI — a company that is itself unprofitable. If OpenAI fails to fulfill that contract or restructures its obligations, a massive chunk of MSFT's forward revenue visibility evaporates.

---

### Inconsistency #2: **GOOGL — Earnings Call Projects Confident Search Growth While 10-K Discloses Active Antitrust Remedies and Declining Safari Search Volumes**

**What the 8-K/earnings call says:** Pichai projected confidence: *"We've been supply constrained even as we've been ramping up our capacity."* Philipp Schindler noted that *"nearly every major vertical actually accelerated in Q4"* for Search revenue, which grew 17% to $224.5B for the full year. Ruth Porat described $175-185B in capex guidance as "an eye towards the future." The press release headline celebrated "$402.8 billion in revenue" and "Google Cloud ending 2025 at an annual run rate of over $70 billion."

**What the 10-K says:** The risk factors section dedicates multiple paragraphs to the DOJ antitrust remedy: on September 2, 2025, the D.C. District Court ordered Google to *end exclusive search distribution agreements* and *share certain search index data with competitors*. The court's order is subject to appeal by both DOJ and Google, with final resolution potentially extending to "2027 or 2028." The EC simultaneously imposed a *€3.0 billion fine* for "self-preferencing" in ad technology. Most damaging: during the remedy proceedings, Apple executive Eddie Cue testified that *search volumes through Safari had declined for the first time in 22 years* — a foundational metric that directly contradicts the earnings call's narrative of broad-based Search acceleration.

**Why it matters:** The earnings call presents Search as an accelerating growth engine. The 10-K discloses that the very distribution mechanism powering that growth (default search deals worth an estimated $20-26B annually to Apple alone) is being legally unwound, and that the underlying search volume on the most valuable distribution channel (iOS Safari) is *declining*. This is arguably the single most important metric for Google's long-term revenue trajectory, and it appeared only in court testimony — not in the press release, not in the earnings call, and not in the MD&A narrative. An investor relying solely on the 8-K would completely miss this.

---

### Inconsistency #3: **META — "Strong Business Performance" Narrative vs. $19.2B Reality Labs Losses and AI Model Execution Risks**

**What the 8-K/earnings call says:** Zuckerberg declared: *"We had strong business performance in 2025"* and expressed excitement about *"advancing personal superintelligence."* Susan Li guided Q1 2026 to $53.5-56.5B and stated that *"despite the meaningful step up in infrastructure investment, we expect to deliver operating income that is above 2025 operating income."* The press release headline led with "Record Revenue" and "24% Year-Over-Year Growth."

**What the 10-K says:** Reality Labs lost *$19.2B in FY2025*, up from $17.7B in FY2024, bringing cumulative 5-year losses to approximately $73B. The 10-K risk factors state that investments in AI and new technologies are subject to "risks related to legal liability, ethical concerns, and competition" and that "the success of these investments is uncertain and could affect long-term financial performance." The MD&A notes total FY2025 expenses of $92.4B with the majority of 2026 expense growth ($162-169B) driven by "infrastructure costs, including third-party cloud spend, higher depreciation, and higher infrastructure operating expenses."

**The specific inconsistency:** Li's claim that 2026 operating income will exceed 2025 levels requires revenue growth of ~20%+ to absorb the guided $70-77B increase in total expenses. The 10-K simultaneously warns that the company's ad targeting capabilities face regulatory constraints — noting that Apple's privacy changes "limit our ability to deliver, target, or measure the effectiveness of advertising" (from VRIN: Google/Apple "affects_advertising_policies by limiting Meta Platforms, Inc's ability to deliver, target, or measure the effectiveness of advertising"). The Q1 2026 guidance also doesn't account for the underwhelming reception to Llama 4, which multiple analysts flagged as having "perceived performance gaps compared to competitors like OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic."

**Why it matters:** The 8-K positions META as a company with unstoppable ad momentum that can absorb massive AI spending. The 10-K reveals a company spending $115-135B in capex while its core advertising business remains vulnerable to platform policy changes (Apple ATT), regulatory intervention (EU DMA/DSA), and its marquee AI product (Llama 4) underperforming peers. The "operating income above 2025" guidance is mathematically tight and assumes no revenue deceleration — a risk the 10-K explicitly warns about but the earnings call dismisses.

---

### Inconsistency #4: **AAPL — "Simply Staggering" iPhone Demand Narrative vs. 10-K Supply Chain Concentration and Tariff Risk Disclosures**

**What the 8-K/earnings call says:** Tim Cook described demand as *"simply staggering"* with iPhone revenue growing 23% in Q1 FY2026. CFO Kevan Parekh raised Q2 guidance to 13-16% growth and guided gross margins to 48-49%. The press release headlined *"Best Quarter Ever"* with $143.8B revenue.

**What the 10-K says:** The risk factor section devotes substantial space to supply chain vulnerability — AAPL has *"entered into agreements for the supply of many components; however, the Company may not be able to extend or renew these agreements on similar terms, or at all."* The 10-K notes AAPL is constrained by *"the availability of the advanced nodes that our SoCs are produced on"* — essentially acknowledging TSMC single-source dependency without naming it. The MD&A section also discloses a new "Tariffs and Other Measures" section noting that *"beginning in the s[econd half of 2025]"* U.S. tariffs began impacting operations.

**The specific gap:** Apple's Greater China revenue declined 4% in FY2025 per the 10-K (VRIN confirmed: *"net_sales_decrease_in_greater_china_for_2025 (4)%"*). Yet the earnings call's "staggering demand" framing made no mention of China weakness. More critically, the Cook-led earnings call never quantified the tariff exposure on the ~85% of iPhones manufactured in China and shipped to global markets, while the 10-K explicitly added tariff risk as a new disclosure category. Parekh's 48-49% gross margin guidance assumes current tariff levels hold — a significant assumption given the 10-K's own warning about "unpredictable shifts in global tariff and trade policies."

**Why it matters:** The delta between "best quarter ever" on the earnings call and "4% China decline + new tariff risk section" in the 10-K is meaningful for margin sustainability. If tariffs escalate, AAPL's guidance becomes unreliable precisely at the moment it's guiding for accelerating growth.

---

### Inconsistency #5: **AMZN — Conservative Revenue Guidance on Earnings Call vs. Aggressive $200B Capex Commitment in Forward-Looking Disclosures**

**What the 8-K/earnings call says:** Olsavsky guided Q1 2026 revenue to $173.5-178.5B (11-15% growth) and explicitly listed macroeconomic risks: *"fluctuations in foreign exchange rates and energy prices, changes in global economic and geopolitical conditions, tariff and trade policies."* Jassy acknowledged that AWS could *"grow faster if we had all the supply that we could take."* The overall tone was measured — confident but hedged.

**What the 10-K/forward filings disclose:** AMZN simultaneously committed to approximately $200B in 2026 capital expenditures. The 10-K risk factors warn that Amazon's *"expansion into new products, services, technologies, and geographic regions subjects us to additional risk"* and that its offerings *"may not be successful."* The filing notes that *"our product and service offerings may not be successful, we may fail to gain or may lose business, and we may be required to increase our spending or lower prices."*

**The inconsistency is directional, not narrative:** This is the only company where the inconsistency actually *favors* the investor. Olsavsky guides revenue conservatively while simultaneously committing $200B to infrastructure — you don't invest $200B if you think you'll only grow 13%. The 10-K risk factors are maximally cautious (deliberately so, for legal protection), while the capex commitment reveals management's *actual* conviction. The 11-15% revenue guide is sandbagged; the $200B capex signals 18-20%+ underlying demand growth expectations. This inconsistency is a *feature* of Amazon's disclosure strategy, not a bug — but it still creates informational asymmetry for investors who only read the press release headline.

---

### Synthesis: What These Inconsistencies Tell You as a Portfolio Manager

The inconsistencies fall into two categories:

**Category 1 — Genuine disclosure risk (MSFT, GOOGL, META):** These three companies have material facts in their 10-K that directly contradict or significantly qualify the optimistic narrative in their 8-K/earnings calls. MSFT's OpenAI-as-competitor classification, GOOGL's declining Safari search volumes amid an active antitrust remedy, and META's $19.2B Reality Labs losses against a "strong performance" narrative all represent cases where an investor relying solely on the earnings press release would have an incomplete picture. These are the inconsistencies most likely to matter in a downturn.

**Category 2 — Strategic framing asymmetry (AAPL, AMZN):** Apple's "staggering demand" vs. China weakness and tariff risk, and Amazon's conservative guidance vs. massive capex commitment, represent management teams strategically choosing what to emphasize. Neither is hiding information — it's all in the 10-K — but the framing in the 8-K creates a different emotional impression than the risk-adjusted picture in the annual filing.

**The most actionable insight:** GOOGL has the widest gap between the 8-K narrative and 10-K reality. The earnings call projects confident Search acceleration while the 10-K discloses that the distribution mechanism powering Search (default agreements) is being legally dismantled, and the underlying volume metric on the highest-value channel (Safari) has turned negative for the first time in over two decades. This is the kind of inconsistency that, historically, precedes revenue estimate revisions — and it's buried in court testimony rather than the press release.
