## Q10: GAAP vs. Non-GAAP Earnings Gap — Which Company Has the Largest, and Should You Be Concerned?

### The Short Answer

**Microsoft has the largest and most concerning GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap** — not because of the absolute dollar magnitude (META's SBC-driven gap is larger in steady-state), but because MSFT is the only company where the *direction* of the gap reverses quarter-to-quarter due to OpenAI equity method accounting, making trend analysis functionally unreliable. In Q2 FY2026, MSFT's GAAP diluted EPS was **$5.16** while non-GAAP was **$4.14** — meaning GAAP was *25% higher* than non-GAAP, the reverse of the typical pattern. One quarter earlier, GAAP was *lower* than non-GAAP due to a $3.1B OpenAI investment loss. This oscillation is unprecedented among the Big Five and represents a genuine analytical hazard.

---

### Company-by-Company Breakdown

#### 1. MSFT — The Most Structurally Concerning Gap

**FY2025 + Recent Quarters Non-GAAP Adjustments:**

| Adjustment | FY2025 (est.) | Q2 FY2026 Impact |
|---|---|---|
| OpenAI investment gains/(losses) | Volatile (~$(1.5)B net loss) | +$7.6B gain ($1.02/share) |
| Amortization of acquired intangibles | ~$7-8B | ~$2B/quarter |
| Income tax effects | Variable | $(0.02)/share |
| **SBC (NOT excluded from non-GAAP)** | **~$30.1B** | **~$8B/quarter** |

**Q2 FY2026 Reconciliation (from VRIN-sourced 10-Q):**
- GAAP diluted EPS: **$5.16**
- Non-GAAP ("adjusted") diluted EPS: **$4.14**
- Gap: GAAP is **24.6% higher** than non-GAAP
- Adjusted net income (non-GAAP): $30,875M vs GAAP net income of ~$38,550M (6-month: $61,708M non-GAAP vs ~$66,200M GAAP)

**What's happening:** MSFT accounts for its OpenAI investment under the equity method, recognizing its share of OpenAI's income/losses through "Other income (expense), net." This created:
- Q1 FY2025: -$523M / -$0.07 per share (OpenAI loss)
- Q2 FY2025: -$939M / -$0.12 per share (OpenAI loss)
- Q1 FY2026: -$3,100M / -$0.42 per share (OpenAI loss)
- Q2 FY2026: +$7,600M / +$1.02 per share (OpenAI gain, driven by recapitalization revaluation)

MSFT excludes these from non-GAAP, then also adds back amortization of acquired intangible assets (primarily from the $69B Activision acquisition in Oct 2023, plus LinkedIn and Nuance).

**Why investors should be concerned:** Three reasons. First, the OpenAI swings make quarter-over-quarter GAAP EPS comparisons meaningless — a 60% GAAP EPS increase in Q2 FY2026 vs. prior year shrinks to 23% on a non-GAAP basis. Second, MSFT does *not* exclude its ~$30B in annual SBC from non-GAAP metrics, which means the non-GAAP figure still includes this significant non-cash expense — but the Activision intangible amortization (~$7-8B/year), which *is* excluded, represents a real economic cost (the purchase price allocated to finite-lived assets). Third, the OpenAI investment creates circular accounting: MSFT funds OpenAI, OpenAI pays MSFT for Azure compute (captured in MSFT revenue), and MSFT recognizes its share of OpenAI's losses (which includes those Azure payments as OpenAI costs). The non-GAAP presentation strips out the loss recognition while keeping the revenue.

#### 2. META — The Largest Absolute SBC Gap

**FY2025 Non-GAAP Adjustments (from VRIN-sourced 10-K cash flow statement):**

| Adjustment | FY2025 Amount |
|---|---|
| Share-based compensation expense | **$20.43B** |
| Depreciation & amortization | $18.62B |
| Deferred income taxes | $18.74B |
| Restructuring charges | ~$1-2B |
| Amortization of acquired intangibles | ~$1-2B |

**The Gap:**
- GAAP net income: **$60.46B**
- SBC alone adds 33.8% to GAAP net income
- If META presented a fully-adjusted non-GAAP net income (excluding SBC + intangible amortization + restructuring), it would be approximately **$82-84B** — roughly **36-39% higher** than GAAP

META's SBC of $20.43B represents **10.2% of total revenue** — the highest SBC-to-revenue ratio among the five companies. The 10-K cash flow reconciliation (from VRIN) explicitly lists SBC as the single largest non-cash adjustment to net income. And the trajectory matters: META is hiring aggressively in AI/engineering roles, which means SBC will continue to grow — the company disclosed an 11% headcount increase from Sep 2024 to Sep 2025 concentrated in "engineering and other technical functions."

**Should investors be concerned?** Moderately. SBC is a real economic cost — it dilutes existing shareholders. META partially offsets this through aggressive buybacks ($46.4B in FY2025), which is the standard Big Tech sterilization strategy. The concern is whether the SBC growth rate (likely 15-20% in FY2026 given AI hiring) will outpace buyback capacity, particularly as META simultaneously commits $115-135B to capex.

#### 3. AMZN — The Free Cash Flow Redefinition Problem

**FY2025 Non-GAAP Adjustments:**

| Adjustment | FY2025 Amount |
|---|---|
| Stock-based compensation | **~$19.2B** (from equity statement via VRIN) |
| Operating lease principal payments (excluded from "FCF") | ~$12-14B |
| Finance lease principal payments | ~$15-20B |

**The Gap — but it's in a different metric:** Amazon doesn't prominently present non-GAAP EPS. Instead, its key non-GAAP metric is "Free Cash Flow," which Amazon defines as operating cash flow minus purchases of property and equipment — **excluding principal repayments on finance and operating leases**. This is the most controversial non-GAAP metric in the Big Five.

From our Q4 analysis: AMZN's GAAP operating cash flow was $139.5B, and the company reported capex of $83.8B in property/equipment purchases. By Amazon's definition, "FCF" = $139.5B - $83.8B = **$55.7B**. But if you include finance lease principal payments (~$15-20B) as true capital commitments, adjusted FCF drops to **$35-40B** — a 28-39% reduction from the headline.

Meanwhile, SBC of ~$19.2B on GAAP net income of $77.7B = 24.7% uplift if excluded. But AMZN doesn't exclude it from their headline metrics.

**Should investors be concerned?** Yes — specifically about the FCF definition. Amazon uses finance leases extensively for data center infrastructure, and excluding those principal payments from "free cash flow" understates the true capital intensity of the business. As AI infrastructure spending ramps to $200B in 2026, more of that may flow through lease structures, widening the gap between Amazon's reported FCF and true free cash generation.

#### 4. GOOGL — The Cleanest Presentation, but Hedging Gains Obscure Operating Trends

**FY2025 Non-GAAP Adjustments:**

| Adjustment | FY2025 Amount |
|---|---|
| Stock-based compensation | **~$24-25B** |
| Hedging gains/(losses) | Variable (~$1-3B) |
| European Commission fines | €3.0B (~$3.3B) |

Alphabet references non-GAAP financial information in its 8-K but primarily reports GAAP metrics. From the VRIN data, GOOGL had SBC of approximately $18.0B credited to additional paid-in capital in FY2025, but the income statement expense was higher (~$24-25B per web sources) due to timing differences in how RSU expenses are recognized vs. shares issued.

The hedging program creates a secondary gap: GOOGL uses revenue hedges on foreign currency, and the 10-Q notes that "constant currency revenues, which exclude the effect of hedging, increased 15% year over year" vs. 16% reported — meaning hedging gains added ~1% to reported growth. Small, but it flatters the top line in a way that could reverse.

**Should investors be concerned?** Less so. GOOGL's non-GAAP presentation is the most transparent of the five. The main risk is the €3.0B EC fine (ad tech antitrust) which is a one-time GAAP hit that GOOGL will treat as non-recurring — but with multiple ongoing antitrust cases (DOJ Search, DOJ ad tech, state AG lawsuits), "non-recurring" legal costs are becoming structurally recurring.

#### 5. AAPL — The Most GAAP-Aligned, Smallest Gap

**FY2025 Non-GAAP Adjustments:**

| Adjustment | FY2025 Amount |
|---|---|
| Stock-based compensation | **~$12-13B** |
| Tax-related items | Variable |

Apple is the most GAAP-focused of the Big Five. It does not prominently present non-GAAP EPS in its earnings press releases. SBC of ~$12-13B on ~$107B GAAP net income represents only an **11-12% gap** — the smallest among all five companies, both in absolute dollars and as a percentage of earnings.

**Should investors be concerned?** No. Apple's GAAP presentation is the gold standard among this group. The only notable non-cash item to watch is D&A (~$11B), which will increase as the company invests more in AI infrastructure, but this flows normally through GAAP.

### The Analyst's Bottom Line

The most actionable insight here isn't about any single company's gap — it's about the **structural shift happening across all five simultaneously**. As AI infrastructure spending explodes ($320B+ combined in 2025, heading toward $500B+ in 2026), depreciation and amortization are becoming the fastest-growing line item in COGS across the Big Five. This depreciation is a GAAP expense that directly reduces reported earnings, but companies are increasingly guiding investors toward non-GAAP metrics that either (a) exclude acquired intangible amortization (MSFT), (b) redefine free cash flow to exclude lease payments (AMZN), or (c) emphasize top-line growth and operating income over net income (GOOGL, META).

The risk for your portfolio: if AI capex doesn't generate proportional revenue within the depreciation window (typically 5-7 years for data center assets), you'll see a GAAP earnings compression across all five names simultaneously — but it may be masked for 2-3 years by non-GAAP presentations that exclude exactly the costs that signal the problem. The company with the highest risk of this dynamic is META (tripling capex to $115-135B while presenting SBC-adjusted earnings) and MSFT (where the OpenAI accounting already makes it impossible to track organic earnings power on a GAAP basis).

Warren Buffett's advice has never been more relevant: *"Stock-based compensation is the most egregious example of accounting that is out of touch with economic reality."* Across these five companies, combined SBC exceeds **$105B annually** — equivalent to the entire GDP of a mid-sized country — and most of it is excluded or de-emphasized in the metrics management wants you to focus on.
