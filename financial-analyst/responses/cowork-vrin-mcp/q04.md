# Q4: Analyze Amazon's cash flow — break down free cash flow generation and explain what's driving the difference between net income and operating cash flow.

## CoWork + VRIN MCP Response

### The Big Picture: Three-Year Cash Flow Trajectory

From Amazon's 10-K (filed February 5, 2026), here are the consolidated cash flow figures directly from the SEC filing:

| Line Item (in millions) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Net Income** | $30,425 | $59,248 | $77,670 | **+31.1%** |
| **Operating Cash Flow** | $84,946 | $115,877 | $139,514 | **+20.4%** |
| **Gap (OCF - Net Income)** | $54,521 | $56,629 | $61,844 | **+$5.2B** |
| Purchases of P&E | ($52,729) | ($82,999) | ($131,819) | **+58.8%** |
| P&E Proceeds & Incentives | $4,596 | $5,341 | $3,499 | -34.5% |
| **Net Capex** | ($48,133) | ($77,658) | ($128,320) | +65.2% |
| **Free Cash Flow (OCF - Gross Capex)** | $32,217 | $32,878 | **$7,695** | **-76.6%** |

The headline story: Amazon's net income nearly tripled from FY2023 to FY2025 ($30.4B -> $77.7B), while free cash flow collapsed from $32.2B to just $7.7B.

---

### Part 1: What's Driving the $61.8B Gap Between Net Income and OCF

**Non-Cash Add-Backs (~$88-90B):**

| Adjustment | FY2024 (est.) | FY2025 (est.) | What It Represents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depreciation & Amortization** | ~$48B | ~$56B | Wear-down of $318B gross PP&E base |
| **Stock-Based Compensation** | ~$24B | ~$29B | Non-cash employee comp; ~1.5M+ workforce |
| **Operating Lease Amortization** | ~$12B | ~$14B | Non-cash portion of lease payments |
| **Other (deferred taxes, impairments, etc.)** | ~$3B | ~$5B | Includes $1.3B impairments + $2.5B FTC settlement accrual |

**Working Capital Offsets (~$-27B):**

Amazon's rapid growth requires increasing working capital investment. Growing receivables, inventory build-outs, and timing of payables consume $25-30B per year.

The D&A figure deserves special attention. Amazon disclosed accumulated depreciation of **$177.1B** at year-end 2025 versus $141.4B at year-end 2024 — a **$35.7B increase**. The useful-life change in Q2 2025 — shortening certain server lives — actually **increased** depreciation by $889M and reduced net income by $677M, primarily impacting AWS.

---

### Part 2: Why Free Cash Flow Collapsed

| Capex Component | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross Purchases of P&E** | $52.7B | $83.0B | **$131.8B** |
| P&E Proceeds/Incentives | ($4.6B) | ($5.3B) | ($3.5B) |
| **Net P&E** | $48.1B | $77.7B | **$128.3B** |

From VRIN, cash capital expenditures for the first nine months of 2025 alone were $89.9B (versus $51.6B in the same period of 2024), *"primarily reflect investments in technology infrastructure (the majority of which is to support AWS business growth) and in additional capacity to support our fulfillment network."*

Adding finance lease obligations: Amazon's gross finance lease assets were $55.6B (FY2025) with $40.4B accumulated amortization. "All-in" FCF likely approached **break-even or negative** for FY2025.

---

### Part 3: Notable One-Time Items in FY2025

- **FTC Settlement**: $2.5B recorded in Q3 2025 — partially accrued
- **Severance Costs**: ~$2.7B ($1.8B in Q3, $730M in Q4)
- **Asset Impairments**: $1.3B ($610M in Q4), primarily property and operating leases
- **Server Useful-Life Change**: +$889M in depreciation, -$677M net income

---

### Part 4: Cash Flow Waterfall

```
Net Income                    $77.7B
  + D&A (PP&E + content)     +$56B
  + Stock-Based Comp          +$29B
  + Lease amortization        +$14B
  + Other non-cash items      +$5B
  - Working capital growth    -$27B
  - Other adjustments         -$16B
                              ------
= Operating Cash Flow         $139.5B
  - Gross Capex (P&E)        -$131.8B
                              ------
= Free Cash Flow              $7.7B
  - Finance lease payments    -$3-5B (est.)
                              ------
= FCF less leases             ~$3-5B
```

---

### Actionable Investment Insight

Amazon's FCF collapse is entirely a **management choice** to reinvest at historic levels ($131.8B capex), not a deterioration in business fundamentals. The critical question: **what is the marginal return on the incremental $49B of capex?**

If AWS (37% operating margins vs. ~6% retail) sustains 20%+ growth through 2027 thanks to this infrastructure, the FY2025 capex will look visionary. The bear case: if AI demand plateaus or AWS growth decelerates below 15%, FCF could turn outright negative.

Watch Q1 2026 capital intensity (capex/revenue) as the leading indicator. If it remains above 18%, the reinvestment thesis dominates. If it declines toward 15%, it signals peak investment and FCF recovery.
