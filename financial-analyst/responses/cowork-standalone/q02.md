### The Standouts

**META is the most aggressive spender relative to revenue** — and it's not close. At 34.7% of revenue going to capex, META is plowing more than a third of every dollar it earns back into infrastructure. That's a 1,200 basis point increase in capex intensity in a single year. Zuckerberg has essentially bet the company on AI infrastructure, and the P&L is starting to reflect it (recall the 80 bps margin compression from Q1 despite 22% revenue growth).

**Amazon is the largest spender in absolute dollars** at $128.3B — more than the GDP of many countries. That 65% year-over-year increase is staggering at this scale. AWS is the engine driving this spend, but Amazon is also investing in logistics automation and AI across its retail operations. At 17.9% of revenue, it's actually lower than META or Microsoft as a percentage, but only because Amazon's revenue base ($717B) is so enormous.

**Microsoft and Alphabet are in a similar band** — both spending 19-23% of revenue on capex, both growing capex in the mid-40% range YoY. Microsoft's spend is heavily concentrated on Azure data centers and the infrastructure backing its Copilot AI suite. Alphabet's spend is split between Google Cloud and the AI infrastructure powering Search, YouTube, and Gemini.

**Apple is the outlier.** At just 2.2% of revenue, Apple's capex is an order of magnitude lower than its peers. Apple actually *decreased* capex by 7%. This reflects Apple's fundamentally different business model — it designs silicon and software but outsources manufacturing to partners like TSMC and Foxconn. Apple's AI strategy (Apple Intelligence) relies more on on-device processing and selective cloud partnerships than building its own massive data center fleet.

### Trajectory Analysis

The capex trajectories tell a clear story about strategic positioning:

**The Hyperscalers (AMZN, MSFT, GOOGL)** are in an arms race. All three operate major cloud platforms (AWS, Azure, GCP) and view AI infrastructure as existential. Their capex growth rates of 43-65% suggest they're still in the acceleration phase, not yet at peak spend. The risk is that they're all building capacity simultaneously, which could lead to oversupply if enterprise AI adoption doesn't ramp as quickly as projected.

**META is the wild card.** It doesn't sell cloud services to third parties — all that infrastructure is for its own AI workloads (recommendation engines, Reels, generative AI features, Llama model training). Spending 35% of revenue on capex without a cloud revenue stream to monetize excess capacity is a fundamentally different risk profile. If AI doesn't drive enough incremental ad revenue, that spend becomes a massive drag.

**Apple is playing a different game entirely.** Its capex-light model means it generates far more FCF per dollar of revenue than any peer. The question is whether this approach is sustainable as AI becomes more compute-intensive, or whether Apple will eventually need to build more of its own cloud infrastructure.

### The Big Picture

Collectively, these five companies spent **$347.1B on capex in FY2025** — up from $221.9B in FY2024, a 56% increase. That's roughly $125 billion in incremental infrastructure investment in a single year, almost entirely AI-driven. To put that in context, it's more than the entire annual GDP of Hungary.

The crucial question for investors: is this spend generating adequate returns? So far, the revenue growth numbers suggest yes — particularly for META and Microsoft, which are seeing strong top-line acceleration alongside the spend. But the FCF compression across the board (as we saw in Q1) means the market is essentially taking it on faith that these investments will pay off over a 3-5 year horizon.
