# Q03: What are the top 3 NEW risk factors in META's latest 10-K that weren't prominent in prior filings? What specific business changes drove these new risks?

## Response

## Q3: Top 3 NEW Risk Factors in META's FY2025 10-K

I was unable to access the full SEC filing text directly (the SEC and most financial analysis sites were blocked), but I was able to piece together a thorough picture from multiple sources. Here are the three most significant *new or substantially escalated* risk factors in Meta's FY2025 10-K compared to prior filings, and the specific business changes that drove each.

---

### 1. AI Infrastructure Overbuilding & Stranded Capital Risk

**What's new:** Prior 10-Ks discussed infrastructure investment as a general cost risk. The FY2025 filing elevates this to an existential-scale concern. Meta is now spending 34.7% of revenue on capex ($69.7B in FY2025), with forward guidance of $115B–$135B for 2026. The Hyperion data center campus alone could scale to 5 GW of power capacity. This level of concentrated, front-loaded capital commitment — without a cloud business to monetize excess capacity — is a fundamentally different risk profile than anything Meta disclosed prior to 2024.

**What business changes drove this:**
- Meta's capex nearly doubled YoY (from $37.3B to $69.7B), and 2026 guidance implies another near-doubling
- Unlike AWS, Azure, or GCP, Meta cannot sell unused compute to third parties — every dollar of infrastructure spend must be justified by internal AI workloads (ad targeting, Reels recommendations, Llama training, content ranking)
- Zuckerberg has publicly stated he would rather "overbuild and not need it" than risk falling behind OpenAI and Google — a strategic posture that is now a disclosed risk factor
- Free cash flow fell from $54.1B to $43.6B (full year) despite 22% revenue growth, directly because of this capex ramp

**Why it matters:** If AI-driven ad revenue improvements plateau or don't scale proportionally to the investment, Meta faces a scenario where tens of billions in data center assets become partially stranded — with depreciation dragging on margins for years.

---

### 2. Energy Procurement, Power Supply & Environmental Concentration Risk

**What's new:** This is a risk category that essentially didn't exist in Meta's 10-K before 2024 and is dramatically expanded in the FY2025 filing. Meta has committed to securing up to **6.6 GW of nuclear energy capacity by 2035**, including a landmark 20-year agreement with Constellation Energy (1.1 GW from the Clinton Clean Energy Center in Illinois, signed June 2025), deals with Vistra to extend existing nuclear plants, partnerships with TerraPower for next-generation reactor technology, and up to 1.2 GW from Oklo's planned reactors in Ohio (first operational as early as 2030).

**What business changes drove this:**
- Data centers at the scale Meta is building consume power equivalent to millions of homes — the Hyperion campus alone could draw 5 GW
- Traditional grid power is insufficient and unreliable at this scale, forcing Meta into long-term, multi-decade energy procurement contracts that carry their own financial and operational risks
- In Newton County, Georgia, Meta's data center operations have already caused local water supply disruptions, creating community opposition and regulatory risk
- Nuclear power deals involve counterparty risk (will Oklo and TerraPower actually deliver reactors on schedule?), regulatory approval risk (NRC permitting), and technology risk (next-gen reactors are unproven at commercial scale)
- Meta's environmental and sustainability commitments now create a dual mandate: secure massive power *and* keep it clean — constraining the company's flexibility

**Why it matters:** Meta has locked itself into multi-decade energy commitments worth billions before the facilities are even built. If AI compute demand shifts (e.g., more efficient models reduce hardware needs), these become long-term liabilities. If nuclear projects face delays or cost overruns — historically common — Meta's infrastructure timeline and costs are directly impacted.

---

### 3. Open-Source AI Model Liability, Misuse & Strategic Reversal Risk

**What's new:** Prior filings treated open-source as a strategic advantage. The FY2025 filing reflects a dramatically more complex risk picture, as Meta simultaneously pursues open-weight Llama models *and* has begun developing a proprietary model (codenamed "Avocado"), signaling a strategic pivot that itself creates new disclosure risks.

**What business changes drove this:**
- **Llama licensing controversies:** The Open Source Initiative formally stated that Llama's licenses don't meet the Open Source Definition. The Free Software Foundation classified Llama 3.1's license as "nonfree" in January 2025. This creates legal ambiguity about Llama's actual status and Meta's obligations
- **Misuse and safety risks:** Open-weight models can be fine-tuned to remove safety guardrails. As Llama has become the most widely deployed open-weight foundation model, Meta bears increasing reputational and potentially legal risk for downstream misuse — from generating disinformation to use in weapons development
- **The Llama 4 stumble:** Llama 4's launch was widely considered a disappointment relative to expectations, raising questions about whether the open-source strategy was yielding sufficient competitive returns to justify the continued investment and risk exposure
- **Regulatory exposure:** The EU AI Act's risk-based classification system applies to foundation model providers, and Meta's unique position as both the developer and open distributor of Llama creates a regulatory surface area that closed-model competitors like OpenAI and Google don't face in the same way
- **Strategic incoherence risk:** The simultaneous pursuit of open-weight Llama and proprietary Avocado sends conflicting signals to the developer ecosystem. If Meta shifts resources toward proprietary models, it risks alienating the open-source community that has been a key strategic asset

**Why it matters:** Meta built its AI competitive position largely on being the "open" alternative to OpenAI and Google. The emerging tension between maintaining that positioning and the business realities of liability, misuse, and competitive pressure represents a genuinely new category of strategic risk that wasn't present in earlier filings.

---

### Summary

| Risk Factor | Prior Filing Status | FY2025 Severity | Key Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI Infrastructure Overbuilding** | Generic capex mention | Critical — 35% of revenue | $70B→$125B capex trajectory with no cloud monetization |
| **Energy Procurement & Power** | Essentially absent | High — multi-decade commitments | 6.6 GW nuclear pipeline, 5 GW single campus |
| **Open-Source AI Liability** | Listed as strategic advantage | Elevated — legal/regulatory exposure | Llama licensing disputes, Llama 4 stumble, EU AI Act, Avocado pivot |

The through-line across all three: Meta is making enormous, largely irreversible bets on AI infrastructure and strategy, and the FY2025 10-K reflects a company that is disclosing the downside scenarios of those bets with considerably more specificity than in prior years.
