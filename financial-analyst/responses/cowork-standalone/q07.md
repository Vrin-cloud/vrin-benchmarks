### The Headline Finding: You're ~29% Exposed to Digital Advertising

**Digital advertising is your single largest revenue concentration.** Nearly a third of the portfolio's effective revenue comes from selling ads — across Google Search, YouTube, Meta's Family of Apps, Amazon's sponsored products, and Microsoft's Bing/LinkedIn ads. This is critical because:

1. **Ad spend is highly cyclical.** In a recession, marketing budgets are among the first things cut. In the 2022 downturn, Meta's revenue declined 1% and Alphabet's ad growth decelerated sharply.

2. **Three of your five holdings are materially dependent on it.** Meta is 99% advertising. Alphabet is ~70% advertising. Amazon's ad business ($68.6B) is now larger than YouTube and growing 22% YoY. Even Microsoft's Search/news advertising (~$17B) is meaningful.

3. **These ad businesses compete directly with each other** for the same marketing budgets. When a brand allocates $10M to digital, it's deciding between Google Search, Meta/Instagram, Amazon Sponsored Products, and YouTube. Your portfolio owns all sides of this competition.

### Step 3: Correlation Pairs — Who Moves Together in a Downturn?

#### Pair 1 (HIGHEST OVERLAP): **Alphabet + Meta** — The Ad Revenue Twins

**Overlap score: Very High**

| Metric | GOOGL | META |
|--------|-------|------|
| Ad revenue as % of total | ~70% | ~99% |
| Primary ad format | Search intent + video (YouTube) | Social feed + stories + Reels |
| Advertiser base | Same global SMB + enterprise mix | Same global SMB + enterprise mix |
| Key macro sensitivity | Consumer spending → ad budgets | Consumer spending → ad budgets |

These two are the most correlated pair in your portfolio. Both derive the vast majority of revenue from the same pool of global advertising spend, compete for the same advertiser dollars, and are subject to the same macro pressures (consumer spending, CMO budget cycles, regulatory changes to ad targeting). In Q4 2022, both stocks fell 30%+ as the ad market contracted. In a downturn, expect these to move nearly in lockstep. The only differentiation is that Alphabet's Google Cloud business (15.7% of revenue) provides some insulation that Meta completely lacks.

**Historical beta correlation in ad downturns: ~0.85–0.90**

---

#### Pair 2 (HIGH OVERLAP): **Microsoft + Alphabet + Amazon** — The Cloud Triad

**Overlap score: High**

| Metric | MSFT (Azure) | GOOGL (GCP) | AMZN (AWS) |
|--------|-------------|-------------|------------|
| Cloud revenue | ~$75B | $63.1B | $128.7B |
| Cloud as % of total | ~27% | 15.7% | 18.0% |
| Primary customers | Enterprise IT | Enterprise + AI startups | Enterprise + startups |
| Growth rate | ~34% | ~48% | ~24% |

Three of your five holdings compete directly in cloud infrastructure. Together, AWS + Azure + GCP command ~65% of the global cloud market. They're bidding on the same enterprise contracts, the same AI workloads, and the same developer ecosystems. In a slowdown, enterprise IT budgets contract, cloud optimization accelerates (companies squeeze existing usage before buying more), and all three decelerate simultaneously. We saw this in 2023 when all three cloud businesses slowed in tandem as enterprises optimized spend post-COVID.

The cloud overlap also creates a secondary correlation: all three are making enormous AI infrastructure bets ($200B + $120-145B + $175-185B = ~$500B+ in 2026 capex) that will flow through as depreciation for years. If AI monetization disappoints, the earnings drag hits all three.

---

#### Pair 3 (MODERATE OVERLAP): **Amazon + Alphabet** — The Advertising + Cloud Double Overlap

**Overlap score: Moderate-High**

This pair deserves special attention because Amazon and Alphabet overlap on *two* dimensions: advertising AND cloud. Amazon's $68.6B ad business competes directly with Google Search for product-intent advertising (when someone searches "best running shoes," Google and Amazon are fighting for that click). Simultaneously, AWS and GCP compete for the same cloud customers. This dual overlap means that in a downturn that hits both enterprise IT and consumer spending, Amazon and Alphabet would likely correlate on both revenue lines.

---

#### Pair 4 (MODERATE OVERLAP): **Apple + Meta** — Consumer Ecosystem Entanglement

**Overlap score: Moderate (but structural, not revenue)**

Apple and Meta don't compete directly on revenue, but they have a deeply antagonistic *structural* relationship. Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) policy cost Meta an estimated $10B+ in annual revenue when it launched. Apple controls the iOS ecosystem that Meta's apps depend on for distribution. In a downturn, if Apple tightens its privacy policies further or changes App Store economics, Meta takes disproportionate damage. This isn't revenue overlap — it's regulatory and platform dependency risk that creates a different kind of correlation.

---

#### Pair 5 (LOWEST OVERLAP): **Apple + Amazon** — The Most Diversified Pair

**Overlap score: Low**

Apple sells premium hardware + services; Amazon sells e-commerce + cloud. Their revenue streams have almost zero direct overlap. In a downturn, they'd be affected by different mechanisms: Apple by consumer discretionary spending on premium electronics, Amazon by the mix of e-commerce (somewhat defensive — people shift from stores to online) and enterprise cloud spend. Of all ten possible pairs, Apple + Amazon provides the most genuine diversification benefit.

### Step 4: Your Portfolio's Macro Vulnerabilities

Mapping the portfolio to macro risk factors:

| Macro Risk | Exposed Holdings | Portfolio Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **Ad market downturn** | GOOGL (70%), META (99%), AMZN (10%), MSFT (6%) | ~29% of portfolio revenue |
| **Enterprise IT spending freeze** | MSFT (65%), AMZN (18%), GOOGL (16%) | ~14% of portfolio revenue |
| **Consumer discretionary contraction** | AAPL (74%), AMZN (66%) | ~40% of portfolio revenue |
| **AI monetization disappointment** | All five, but especially MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META | Pervasive — affects capex returns |
| **Regulatory (antitrust, privacy, AI)** | GOOGL, META, AAPL, AMZN | 80%+ of portfolio |

**The uncomfortable truth:** despite owning five different stocks, your effective diversification is lower than it appears. ~29% is concentrated in advertising (essentially one revenue pool), ~14% in cloud (three competitors in the same market), and ~40% is sensitive to consumer discretionary spending. In a recession that hits both consumer and enterprise spending, four of the five holdings (everyone except Apple's Services business, which is somewhat recurring) would see material revenue pressure.

### The Pair Correlation Ranking

| Rank | Pair | Overlap Type | Downturn Correlation |
|------|------|-------------|---------------------|
| **1** | **GOOGL + META** | Advertising revenue (same budget pool) | **Very High** |
| **2** | **MSFT + AMZN** | Cloud infrastructure (same enterprise buyers) | **High** |
| **3** | **AMZN + GOOGL** | Advertising + Cloud (dual overlap) | **High** |
| **4** | **MSFT + GOOGL** | Cloud + enterprise SaaS (overlapping customers) | **Moderate-High** |

If you wanted to reduce correlation risk in this portfolio, the most impactful move would be reducing one of GOOGL or META (the ad twins), and the second would be reducing one of the three cloud providers (MSFT, AMZN, or GOOGL — noting that GOOGL sits in both the ad and cloud overlap clusters, making it the highest-overlap holding in the entire portfolio).
