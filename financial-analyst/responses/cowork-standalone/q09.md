# Q09: Are there any notable inconsistencies between what management highlighted in their earnings press release (8-K) versus what's disclosed in the 10-K risk factors or MD&A for any of these companies?

## Response

## Q9: 8-K Press Release vs. 10-K Disclosures — The Narrative Gaps

This is one of the most important things an investor can do: compare the confident, curated story management tells on earnings night (the 8-K press release and call) versus the legally mandated, lawyer-reviewed risk disclosures in the 10-K (filed weeks or months later). The inconsistencies reveal what management *knows* but doesn't *emphasize*. I found meaningful gaps at all five companies, but three stand out as particularly notable.

---

### 1. AMAZON — The Most Glaring Inconsistency

#### What the 8-K Says:
Andy Jassy's Q4 2025 press release opens with "strong momentum and growth across Amazon" and highlights $716.9B in revenue (+12.4%), $80.0B in operating income (+16.6%), and AWS reaccelerating to 24% growth. The tone is triumphant — Amazon is firing on all cylinders.

#### What the 10-K/MD&A Reveals:

**Free cash flow collapsed 71% — and the press release buries it.**

The 8-K mentions operating cash flow grew 20% to $139.5B (true, and sounds great), but the free cash flow figure of $11.2B appears deep in the financial statements with minimal commentary. This represents a 71% decline from $38.2B the prior year. Amazon's own definition of FCF — which it has historically trumpeted as its preferred performance metric — is now conspicuously de-emphasized in the press release narrative.

For years, Amazon's investor letters and press releases led with "free cash flow increased to…" as the primary financial highlight. In the Q4 2025 release, FCF is mentioned but not highlighted. Meanwhile, the $200B capex guidance for 2026 means Amazon is on track to potentially go **free cash flow negative** in 2026 — something Evercore flagged as a "red flag" for hyperscalers broadly.

**The 10-K's MD&A acknowledges what the press release glosses over:** that purchases of property and equipment increased $50.7B YoY, "primarily reflecting investments in artificial intelligence." The 10-K risk factors disclose that "we may not realize the anticipated benefits of our significant investments" and that "our investments in technology, including AI, may not result in proportionate revenue or profit increases." The press release contains no such caveats.

**Severity: High.** This is a textbook case of selective emphasis. The same management team that historically centered its narrative around FCF is now pivoting to operating income and revenue growth precisely because FCF tells a less flattering story.

### 2. ALPHABET — The Antitrust Omission

#### What the 8-K Says:
Pichai's Q4 2025 press release celebrates "strong results across the board" — 18% revenue growth, Google Cloud up 48%, YouTube exceeding $60B annually, and 750M Gemini monthly active users. The release is pure momentum narrative.

#### What the 10-K Reveals:

**The search default agreements that generate ~$26B/year are under existential threat — and the press release says almost nothing about it.**

In September 2025, Judge Mehta issued his remedies order in *United States v. Google*: Google is prohibited from entering exclusive search default agreements of more than one year. This directly targets the $20B+ annual payment to Apple to be Safari's default search engine — which the court found to be the mechanism through which Google maintained its illegal monopoly.

The 10-K risk factors dedicate extensive language to this: the potential loss of default agreements, the requirement to share anonymized search data with competitors, restrictions on exclusive AI (Gemini) distribution deals, and the separate ad-tech antitrust case that found Google guilty and could still lead to a **structural breakup of Google's advertising technology business**.

On the earnings call, Pichai's only reference was a bland statement about "continuing to engage constructively with regulators." No quantification of the potential revenue impact. No discussion of how the business would adapt if the Apple deal — worth roughly a quarter of Google's operating income flow-through — is restructured or eliminated.

**The compounding risk the 10-K flags but the 8-K ignores:** Judge Mehta also restricted exclusive AI distribution deals. This means Google can't replicate its search default playbook with Gemini — precisely the strategy it would need to defend against AI competitors. The 10-K acknowledges that AI could "cannibalize" existing search revenue, but the press release frames AI purely as a growth driver ("products built on generative AI models grew nearly 400%"). These two statements can both be true, but presenting only the positive side creates a misleading picture.

**Severity: Very High.** The gap between "strong results across the board" and "we may lose $26B/year in default agreement revenue and face a potential breakup of our ad-tech business" is the largest narrative inconsistency across all five companies.

### 3. META — The Capex-to-Profitability Bridge

#### What the 8-K Says:
Zuckerberg: "We had strong business performance in 2025. I'm looking forward to advancing personal superintelligence for people around the world in 2026." Revenue grew 22%, operating income hit $83.3B. The press release guides to FY2026 operating income "above FY2025 levels."

#### What the 10-K Reveals:

**The 10-K discloses that Meta has no concrete mechanism to monetize $125B in annual AI infrastructure spend beyond advertising improvements — and acknowledges that the spend could be stranded.**

The 8-K implies a clean narrative: spend $125B → AI improves ad targeting → revenue grows → operating income grows. But the 10-K risk factors tell a different story:

- Meta acknowledges that its open-weight AI models (Llama) generate essentially zero direct revenue and carry increasing liability risk
- The energy procurement commitments (6.6 GW nuclear, 20-year contracts) create multi-decade fixed obligations that the 8-K never mentions
- The 10-K flags that Reality Labs lost ~$17B in FY2025 (estimated), but the press release lumps it into a single line of segment reporting without commentary on when losses will moderate
- The 10-K discloses that the Llama 4 launch underperformed expectations and that Meta is developing a proprietary model (Avocado) — suggesting the open-source strategy that the 8-K continues to champion may be failing

The most notable gap: the 8-K guides to FY2026 total expenses of $162–169B while also promising operating income above $83.3B. Simple math says this requires revenue of **$245B+ minimum** — a 22% growth rate sustained on a larger base with no guarantee that AI-driven ad improvements will scale linearly with infrastructure spend. The 10-K explicitly warns they "may not realize the anticipated benefits" of AI investments. The 8-K guidance implicitly assumes they will.

**Severity: High.** The inconsistency isn't factual (no numbers conflict) — it's in the framing. The 8-K presents an investment story where massive capex leads to proportional returns. The 10-K acknowledges the very real possibility that it doesn't.

### 4. APPLE — The China/Tariff Soft-Pedal

#### What the 8-K Says:
Tim Cook: record $143.8B quarter, iPhone up 23%, Greater China up 38%. Apple Intelligence is driving "incredible customer satisfaction." The tone is that Apple's global business is thriving.

#### What the 10-K Reveals:

**The 10-K devotes extensive new language to tariff risk that the press release barely mentions.**

The FY2025 10-K (filed November 2025) introduces significantly expanded disclosure on tariffs and trade policy. New tariffs announced in early 2025 apply to imports from China, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam, and the EU. Apple estimates these tariffs could add $900M to costs in a single quarter and potentially $10B+ annually to iPhone production costs. The 10-K warns that "geopolitical tensions" and "trade disputes" could have a "material adverse effect" on the business.

On earnings calls, Cook frames tariffs as "manageable" and emphasizes supply chain diversification (India, Vietnam). But the 10-K's language is far more cautious — acknowledging that China remains ~19% of revenue and that government restrictions on iPhone use by Chinese state employees, combined with fierce Huawei competition, represent genuine market share risk.

**The AI gap:** Cook promotes Apple Intelligence enthusiastically on calls, but the 10-K introduces new risk language about AI — specifically that "new products, services and technologies may replace or supersede existing offerings and may produce lower revenues and lower profit margins." This is a remarkable admission buried in risk factors: Apple is acknowledging that its AI features might not command the pricing power of the products they enhance or replace.

**Severity: Moderate.** The tariff risk is disclosed in the 10-K but minimized on calls. The AI risk disclosure is genuinely new and worth watching.

### 5. MICROSOFT — The Most Consistent (But Not Without Gaps)

#### What the 8-K Says:
Nadella: "We are only at the beginning phases of AI diffusion, and already Microsoft has built an AI business that is larger than some of our biggest franchises." Revenue growth of 17%, operating income up 21%, Azure at 38%.

#### What the 10-K Reveals:

Microsoft is the most narratively consistent of the five — the 8-K and 10-K tell largely compatible stories. However, there are two notable gaps:

**Depreciation acceleration is understated in the press release.** Microsoft's capex of $64.6B in FY2025 (and ~$145B guided for FY2026) will create an enormous depreciation wave. The 10-K MD&A acknowledges that depreciation expense is growing faster than revenue growth and will accelerate further as AI infrastructure comes online. On the earnings call, CFO Amy Hood mentioned that depreciation "will be a meaningful headwind" — but the press release leads with operating income growth, not the fact that the gross margin declined 100 bps from 69.8% to 68.8% partly due to this depreciation effect.

**Copilot monetization is more uncertain than it appears.** The 8-K highlights 100M Copilot MAUs as a triumph. But the 10-K risk factors acknowledge that "AI products and services may not achieve the market acceptance or generate the revenue we anticipate" and that customers may "reduce or eliminate their use" of AI products. The 10-K also notes competitive risk from open-source alternatives (notably Meta's Llama) that could undercut Copilot's pricing. None of this appears in the press release.

**Severity: Low-Moderate.** Microsoft's narrative discipline keeps the gap smaller than peers, but the depreciation headwind and Copilot uncertainty deserve more prominent disclosure.

### The Broader Pattern

Every company does this to some degree — the 8-K is a marketing document (legally disclosed but narratively curated), while the 10-K is a compliance document (legally mandated and lawyer-approved). The SEC requires risk factors to be comprehensive, which means the 10-K will always be more cautious. But the *degree* of inconsistency varies, and the cases above go beyond normal presentation differences into material narrative gaps that could mislead investors who only read the press release.

The single most actionable takeaway: **always read the 10-K risk factors and MD&A *after* reading the earnings press release.** The 8-K tells you what management wants you to believe. The 10-K tells you what the lawyers made them say. The truth is usually somewhere in between.
