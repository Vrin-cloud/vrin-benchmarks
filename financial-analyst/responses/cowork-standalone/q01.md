### Operating Income & Margins

| Company | FY2025 Op. Income | FY2025 Op. Margin | FY2024 Op. Income | FY2024 Op. Margin | Margin Change |

Microsoft commands the highest operating margin at 45.6% and expanded it by 100 basis points — the best margin improvement in the group. META has the second-highest margin at 41.4%, but it actually compressed by 80 bps as capex-related costs (depreciation from massive AI infrastructure buildout) hit the P&L. Alphabet's margin was essentially flat. Amazon showed solid margin expansion on an absolute basis (+50 bps), continuing its post-2023 efficiency story.

### Free Cash Flow (Operating Cash Flow minus CapEx)

| Company | FY2025 FCF | FY2024 FCF | YoY Change | FCF Margin |
|---------|-----------|-----------|------------|------------|
| **AAPL** | $98.8B | $108.8B | **-9.2%** | 23.7% |
| **MSFT** | $71.6B | $74.1B | **-3.3%** | 25.4% |
| **GOOGL** | $73.3B | $72.8B | **+0.7%** | 18.2% |
| **AMZN** | $11.2B | $38.2B | **-70.7%** | 1.6% |
| **META** | $46.1B | $54.1B | **-14.7%** | 22.9% |

This is the most striking table. **Every company except Alphabet saw FCF decline**, and Amazon's FCF cratered by 71% — from $38.2B to just $11.2B — as capital expenditures surged from $77.7B to $128.3B (a 65% increase), almost entirely driven by AI/cloud infrastructure buildout. META's capex nearly doubled from $37.3B to $69.7B, crushing FCF by 15%. Even Apple, despite generating the highest absolute FCF, saw a 9% decline as capex rose 35% while operating cash flow dipped.

The AI capex cycle is the dominant story here. These companies are collectively pouring hundreds of billions into data centers and GPUs, and it's visibly compressing free cash flow across the board.

### Strongest Overall YoY Improvement

**META wins on revenue growth** (22.2%), but if we're looking at the most *balanced* improvement across all three metrics, **Microsoft** makes the strongest case:

- 14.9% revenue growth (second-best after META)
- 100 bps of operating margin expansion (best in class)
- 17.4% operating income growth to $128.5B
- FCF decline was the mildest among the large spenders at just -3.3%

However, I want to flag a tension: if your primary lens is top-line momentum, META is the clear winner. If your lens is profitability discipline amid the AI capex cycle, Microsoft is spending aggressively ($64.6B in capex) while still expanding margins — that's harder to do.

**Amazon is the caution flag.** The 71% FCF decline is severe. Yes, they're investing for the future, but at $128.3B in capex on $716.9B in revenue, they're spending at a rate that demands AWS continue its growth trajectory. If cloud growth decelerates, that capex commitment becomes a serious drag.
